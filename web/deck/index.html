The problem

Agents are getting wallets. The policy is still written by hand.

  • Coinbase, Privy, Turnkey, x402 all ship a policy engine: caps, allowlists, sessions.
  • Nobody ships the policy. A developer writes allowlists once and never measures them.
  • Too loose: one injected line in a PDF drains the account.
  • Too tight: a human is back in the loop for every payment, which the agent was meant to remove.
  • No chargeback on a settled stablecoin transfer. The control has to exist before the money leaves.
Everyone who gives an agent a wallet hits the same fork. The engines are good. The policy inside them is a guess. And unlike cards, there is no dispute process after the fact.

Two structural properties do the work: the agent can never call its own account, and refusals are legible before they cost anything. The test suite runs every scenario through both simulate and execute and fails if they ever disagree.

What we build nextin development, not shipped

Compile the policy from what the agent actually does.

Compile

Traces in, policy out

Shadow-mode tool calls and payments become a one-page readable policy plus the enforcement artifact: Rein's account today, Coinbase, Privy and Turnkey policy JSON next.

Measure

Two numbers, with denominators

Coverage: how often honest actions get blocked on held-out traces. Catch rate: how many injected attacks are refused. A policy that cannot state them is a guess.

Prove

A receipt a counterparty can check

Merchant, treasury owner or insurer verifies the agent was inside an attested policy before accepting settlement, without trusting the operator.

This is the company. Rein today is the enforcement half. The compile half is the rule learner I built for ARC-AGI-3 pointed at agent traces. It sits on top of the wallets people already use rather than competing with them.

Prompt injection is distribution shift. A learner that refuses where it has not seen enough fails in the safe direction: a compiled policy is never looser than a hand-written one.

The wallet vendors are allies, not competitors: they own the engine, we fill it. The enterprise agent-security vendors are the closest analogue and they are off-chain and vendor-enforced, so no counterparty can check anything. The obvious question is why Coinbase does not add this. Answer: the policy must be portable and verifiable across vendors, and inducing it with abstention is a research problem, not a feature.
