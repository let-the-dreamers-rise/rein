Rein terms

Last updated: 2 October 2026

Rein checks payments before you approve them. It reads public blockchain data and tells you when a payment looks unusual compared with your own history. These terms cover the Rein website, the rein-wallet package, the Safe app and the GitHub Action.

What Rein does. Rein reads public data (your Safe's queue and history, public transactions) and gives you a result such as "Looks normal", "Check first" or "Don't sign yet". It can warn you or hold a payment for a person to approve. It can't send, move or freeze your money, and it never asks for your private keys or seed phrase.

What Rein doesn't promise. Rein is a second opinion. It can miss things, and it can warn about payments that are fine. "Looks normal" means nothing matched Rein's checks; it doesn't mean a payment is safe. You decide whether to sign, and you're responsible for the payments you approve.
